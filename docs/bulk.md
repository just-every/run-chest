# Selected items and archive downloads

Select files and folders in a browser listing or search, then choose **Actions for selected items**. The selection can include different folders. Choose move, copy, share, trash, restore or download. Move and copy use the destination controls; share uses a person or group and a role. A selected folder represents its tree. Selecting both a folder and its descendant does not execute the descendant twice.

Chest stores a private job for the initiating person. Its page lists successes, refusals and pending items separately. Keep that page open to process remaining items, or return to **Your jobs** and resume later. Cancellation stops remaining work and keeps completed changes. Browser continuation requires a valid signed-in session; no service impersonates someone after they leave. This is a resumable browser workflow, not a background daemon.

Every item uses current rights and sharing policy when processed. Grant removal, a role downgrade, a destination refusal or policy change can produce a partial result. Items the person can no longer see are unnamed. The existing move/copy/share/trash/restore implementations enforce their normal rules and retain their usual audit events. Each mutation and its deterministic job receipt commit in one database transaction. Competing continuations cannot duplicate a copy or move, and a connection failure after commit is reconciled from the receipt. An uncertain failure stays pending rather than being called a refusal or success.

**Download selection ZIP** streams original object chunks into an uncompressed ZIP64 archive. The server holds entry metadata and the central directory, not file bodies or the complete archive. All archives use ZIP64, including small ones. Paths have a `Selection N` wrapper to distinguish different selected roots; original names and the folder hierarchy follow it. Empty accessible folders are retained. The last entry, `chest-manifest.json`, lists included paths, exact immutable version IDs, sizes and SHA-256 values, plus exclusions and their reasons. Inaccessible exclusions use selection positions without names or paths. Every original SHA-256 and ZIP CRC is recomputed while streaming; a mismatch interrupts the archive before a completed manifest.

The selected versions are captured when the job is created. Every archive entry, and each original-data chunk, rechecks the live session, current rights, no-download policy and cancellation. A failure after an entry header has left interrupts the archive; it never becomes a successful partial file. If connectivity breaks, request a new archive from the same job. ZIP delivery does not claim the recipient saved or verified its bytes. Run outcomes describe item eligibility; the manifest describes the particular generated archive, since rights can change between the two.

The HTTP doors are `POST /api/chest/bulk` with `action`, `items` (an ID array), and the normal destination/sharing fields; `GET /api/chest/bulk`; `GET /api/chest/bulk/:id`; `POST /api/chest/bulk/:id/run` (20 remaining items by default, `limit` 1–50); `POST /api/chest/bulk/:id/cancel`; and `GET /api/chest/bulk/:id/archive`. Private mutations require the current session and same-origin request. Refused items are terminal for that job; make a new selection after fixing their rights. Stored jobs are limited to 1,000 selected roots and 10,000 expanded archive entries. Existing bounded folder-copy/tree limits also apply.

BULK-001 remains **partial**. This slice provides browser selection, durable resumed mutations and streaming ZIP64. Native desktop and mobile application selection, guest-folder and file-request recipient archive grants, a 6 GB guest download, arbitrary huge trees and an autonomous server-side worker are not delivered or proven here. Browser proof at phone width is not a native mobile application. The public-door test exercises a 200-file selection, five no-download exclusions, 197-success/three-refusal move and share results, restart and concurrent continuation. It does not establish the contract's huge-archive or native-client acceptance journeys.
